## Grammar

### 1. -는다고/ㄴ다고/다고 하던데

**Meaning and Usage**
- This is used with an action or descriptive verb to speak about something the speaker has heard in the past to confirm its validity or to ask a question about that statement. It is also used to propose an action, make a suggestion, or give a command.

**Form**
- If the action verb stem ends in a consonant, ‘-는다고 하던데’ is used and if it ends in a vowel, ‘-ㄴ다고 하던데’ is used. Following a descriptive verb stem, ‘-다고 하던데’ is used.

| Preceding word stem | Combine with |
|---------------------|--------------|
| Consonant (action verb) | -는다고 하던데 |
| Vowel (action verb) | -ㄴ다고 하던데 |
| Descriptive verb | -다고 하던데 |

**Examples**
- 먹는다고 하던데, 읽는다고 하던데
- 간다고 하던데, 배운다고 하던데
- 싸다고 하던데, 많다고 하던데

**Examples**
- 안나 씨가 다음 주에 고향에 돌아간다고 하던데 송별회를 준비할까요? (I heard that Anna is returning to her hometown next week. Should we prepare a farewell party?)
- 새로 나온 영화가 재미있다고 하던데 보셨어요? (I heard that the new movie is fun. Have you seen it?)
- 리에 씨가 일본어 선생님이라고 하던데 리에 씨에게 물어보세요. (I heard Rie is a Japanese teacher, so you should ask her.)
- 오늘은 수리 센터가 문을 열었다고 하던데 오후에 한번 가보세요. (I heard that the repair center is open today. You should stop by in the afternoon.)

**가:** 정희 씨가 다음 달에 결혼한다고 하던데 정말이에요? (A: I heard that Jeonghee is getting married next month. Is that true?)

**나:** 네, 요즘 결혼 준비 때문에 아주 바쁘대요. (B: Yes, she said she’s very busy these days with wedding preparations.)

**가:** 요즘 계속 목이 아파요. (A: My throat constantly hurts these days.)

**나:** 생강차를 마시면 좋다고 하던데 한번 드셔보세요. (B: I heard that ginger tea is good for throat aches. You should give it a try.)

### 2. -는대로

**Meaning and Usage**
- This is used together with an action verb to denote that as soon as a certain action is taken, something will immediately follow. This isn’t used to refer to what has happened in the past. It is used to promise that something will happen in the future or make a request.

**Examples**
- 집에 도착하는대로 연락할게요. (I’ll call you as soon as I arrive home.)
- 문자 메시지를 보는대로 저에게 전화해 주세요. (Call me as soon as you see this text message.)
- 퇴근하는대로 약속 장소로 가겠습니다. (I will head over to our meeting location as soon as I get off from work.)

**저는 학교를 졸업하는대로 일을 시작할 거예요.** (I’m going to start working as soon as I graduate.)

This is used with an action or descriptive verb to acknowledge that the preceding statement is true.

**가:** 오늘 언제 집에 올 거니? 빨리 와서 엄마 좀 도와줘. (A: When are you coming home today? Come quickly and give Mom a hand.)

**나:** 네, 수업이 끝나는대로 집에 올게요. (B: All right. I’ll head home as soon as classes are over.)

**가:** 제가 보낸 메일은 확인하셨어요? (A: Have you checked the email I sent?)

**나:** 아니요, 확인하는대로 문자를 보내겠습니다. (B: No, as soon as I check it, I’ll send you a text message.)

## Grammar

### 1. -기는 하다

**Meaning and Usage**
- This is used with an action or descriptive verb to acknowledge that the preceding statement is true.

**Examples**
- 저는 매운 음식을 먹기는 하지만 좋아하지 않아요. (I do eat spicy food, but I don’t particularly like it.)
- 한나 씨를 알기는 해도 친하지는 않아요. (I do know Hannah, but we’re not close.)
- 어제 백화점에서 본 옷이 비싸기는 한데 사고 싶어요. (The clothes I saw yesterday are expensive, but I still want to buy them.)
- 시간이 없어서 못 갔지만 저도 어제 모임에 가고 싶기는 했어요. (I couldn’t go because I didn’t have time, but I did want to go to yesterday’s meeting.)

**가:** 회사 일이 힘들지요? (A: Your office work is hard, right?)

**나:** 힘들기는 하지만 배우는 것도 많고 재미있어요. (B: It is hard, but there’s so much to learn and it’s also fun.)

**가:** 한국에서 혼자 사니까 외롭지 않아요? (A: Isn’t it lonesome living in Korea by yourself?)

**나:** 가족과 같이 살지 않아서 외롭기는 한데 친구들이 있어서 괜찮아요. (B: It’s lonely living without my family, but it’s all right since I have my friends.)

### 2. -다니

**Meaning and Usage**
- This is used with an action or descriptive verb to express the emotion felt by the speaker after seeing or hearing the preceding statement.

**Examples**
- 그렇게 건강하던 사람이 병에 걸리다니 믿을 수 없어요. (I can’t believe that a person so healthy could be sick with a disease.)
- 나하고 만나기로 약속해 놓고 안 오다니 너무 심한 거 아니야? (I can’t believe you promised to meet me and didn’t show up. Isn’t that a bit harsh?)
- 지금까지 모은 돈이 그렇게 많다니 정말 대단하네요. (I can’t believe you’ve saved up that much money until now. It’s amazing.)
- 어제 산 티셔츠가 한 벌에 100만원이라니 그게 정말이에요? (I can’t believe that the T-shirts you bought yesterday were 1,000,000 won each. Is that true?)

**가:** 내일 서울의 낮 기온이 40도래요. (A: They say it’s 40 degrees tomorrow in Seoul.)

**나:** 40도라니 그게 정말이에요? (B: 40 degrees? Is that true?)

**가:** 로니 씨가 열심히 준비한 시험에 떨어졌대. (A: Loni failed the test he was studying so hard for.)

**나:** 정말? 그렇게 열심히 준비했는데 시험에 떨어지다니 말도 안 돼. (B: Really? I can’t believe he failed when he prepared so hard for it.)

**Note:** This expression is followed by a confirmation or an expression of surprise or amazement.

## Grammar

### 1. -을/ㄹ 만하다

**Meaning and Usage**
- This is used with an action verb to state that it is all right to carry out a certain action or to reinforce that a given action is worth it.

**Examples**
- 요즘 상영하고 있는 영화 중에서는 ‘아름다운 꿈’이 볼 만해요. (The recent movie “Beautiful Dream” is worth watching.)
- 외국인이 읽을 만한 한국 소설 책 좀 추천해 주세요. (Please recommend some Korean novels that are worth reading by foreigners.)
- 이 정희 씨가 이번에 과장이 됐는데 그 정도 실력이면 될 만하죠. (Jeonghee Lee was promoted as Director, and given her abilities, she deserves it.)
- 휴대전화가 좀 오래되기는 했지만 아직 쓸 만해요. (This cell phone is pretty old, but it’s still usable.)

**가:** 한국 음식 중에서 뭐가 제일 맛있어요? (A: What's the best among Korean foods?)

**나:** 불고기가 먹을 만해요. 한번 드셔보세요. (B: Bulgogi is worth eating. You should try it out.)

**가:** 정우 씨가 이번에 장학금을 받았대요. (A: I heard Jeongwoo received the scholarship.)

**나:** 받을 만해요. 진짜 열심히 공부했어요. (B: He deserves it. He studied really diligently.)

**Note:** This cannot be used with a descriptive verb.

### 2. -는답니다/ㄴ답니다/답니다

**Meaning and Usage**
- This is used with an action or descriptive verb to let someone know something you think they don’t know.

**Form**
- If the action verb stem ends in ‘ㄹ’ or a vowel, ‘-ㄴ답니다’ is used. If it ends in a consonant, ‘-는답니다’ is used. Following a descriptive verb stem, ‘-답니다’ is used.

| Preceding word stem | Combine with |
|---------------------|--------------|
| Consonant (action verb) | -는답니다 |
| ㄹ, vowel (action verb) | -ㄴ답니다 |
| Descriptive verb | -답니다 |

**Examples**
- 받는답니다, 먹는답니다
- 판답니다, 모인답니다
- 많답니다, 바쁘답니다

**Examples**
- 제 동생은 노래를 아주 잘 부른답니다. (My younger sibling is really good at singing.)
- 된장을 즐겨 먹으면 오래 살 수 있답니다. (If you enjoy eating doenjang, you’ll live a long life.)
- 한라산은 한국에서 두 번째로 높은 산이랍니다. (Mount Halla is the second highest mountain in Korea.)
- 이 학교는 역사가 130년이나 되었답니다. (This school has a 130-year history.)

**가:** 경주는 어떤 곳이에요? (A: What kind of place is Gyeongju?)

**나:** 경주는 역사 도시로 유명하답니다. (B: It’s known as the city of history.)

**가:** 이번에 제 남편이 회사 사장이 되었답니다. (A: You know, my husband became the head of the company recently.)

**나:** 축하드립니다. (B: Congratulations.)

**Note:** It can be used to express closeness or boast about something.

## Grammar

### 1. -을/ㄹ 뿐만 아니라

**Meaning and Usage**
- This is used with an action or descriptive verb to add something to the preceding content.

**Examples**
- 배우 박재성은 연기를 잘할 뿐만 아니라 노래도 잘한대요. (Not only is the actor Jaeseong Park good at acting, I heard he’s also a good singer.)
- 이튼 씨는 성격이 좋을 뿐만 아니라 일도 잘해서 인기가 아주 많아요. (Ethan is very popular because not only does he have a good personality, he’s great at his work, too.)
- 학교 도서관에는 전공책 뿐만 아니라 다른 종류의 책도 많아요. (There are many different types of books in the library in addition to books related to your major.)
- 작년 겨울은 아주 추웠을 뿐만 아니라 눈도 많이 내렸어요. (Not only was it very cold last winter, there was a lot of snow, too.)

**가:** 슈테판 씨, 한국의 비빔밥은 어떤 음식이에요? (A: What kind of food is bibimbap in Korea, Stephan?)

**나:** 맛있을 뿐만 아니라 영양도 아주 풍부한 음식이에요. (B: Not only is it tasty, it’s very nutritious, too.)

**가:** 마이클 씨, 이튼 씨가 그렇게 똑똑하다면서요? (A: Michael, I heard Ethan is really smart. Is that so?)

**나:** 네, 책을 많이 읽을 뿐만 아니라 경험도 많아서 모르는 게 없는 것 같아요. (B: Yes. He not only reads a lot of books, he also has a lot of experience, so it seems like there’s nothing he doesn’t know.)

### 2. -이며/며

**Meaning and Usage**
- This is used with a noun to list two or more similar items and contents and connect them to the predicate.

**Examples**
- 제가 과자며 과일이며 음료수까지 다 사 놓았어요. (I’ve already bought everything from cookies to fruit to beverages.)
- 레오 씨는 요리며 운동이며 못하는 것이 없네요. (Leo is good at everything, including cooking and sports.)
- 우리 기숙사에는 식당이며 헬스클럽이며 없는 것이 없어요. (Our dormitory has everything from a cafeteria to a health club.)
- 태균 씨는 성격이며 외국어 실력이며 모자란 점이 없는 사람이에요. (Taegyun lacks nothing, from foreign language ability to a good personality.)

**가:** 새로 이사한 하숙집은 어때요? (A: How’s the new boarding house?)

**나:** 넓은 방이며 아주머니가 만드신 맛있는 음식이며 마음에 들지 않는 게 없어요. (B: I like everything about it from its spacious room to the delicious food the homeowner makes me.)

**가:** 제주도는 어땠어요? (A: How was Jeju Island?)

**나:** 음식이며 볼거리며 모든 게 다 좋았어요. (B: Everything was great, from the food to the sights to see.)

## Grammar

### 1. 만 못하다

**Meaning and Usage**
- This is used with a noun to compare two items, specifically to state that the preceding item is not as good as the following item.

**Examples**
- 새 노트북이 빠르기는 한데 디자인은 제가 쓰던 노트북만 못해요. (The new laptop is fast, but the design doesn’t compare to my previous laptop.)
- 식당 음식이 맛있지만 어머니가 만들어주신 음식만 못해요. (The food at the restaurant is delicious, but it doesn’t compare to mom’s homemade food.)
- 가격은 비슷한데 배달 도시락이 편의점 도시락만 못한 것 같아요. (The prices are similar, but delivered lunch boxes aren’t as tasty as convenience store lunch boxes.)
- 숙박비가 비싼 호텔이 시설은 좋아도 편하기는 내집만 못해요. (Pricey hotels may have nice facilities, but it’s not as comfortable as my own home.)

**가:** 이 영화는 유명한 소설을 영화로 만든 거라면서요? (A: I heard that this movie was based on a famous novel, right?)

**나:** 네, 그런데 영화가 책만 못한 것 같네요. (B: Yes, but the movie isn’t half as good as the novel.)

**가:** 숙박 시설을 예약하려고 하는데 콘도가 좋을까? 펜션이 좋을까? (A: I’m trying to reserve lodging. Do you think a condo would be better or a pension?)

**나:** 조용한 분위기를 원하는 사람한테는 콘도가 펜션만 못할 것 같아. (B: If you like a quiet ambiance, condos can’t compare to pensions.)

### 2. -기도 하다

**Meaning and Usage**
- This is used with an action or descriptive verb to state that both the contents of the preceding and following clauses are true or have happened.

**Examples**
- 이 휴대폰은 가볍기도 하고 예쁘기도 해서 인기가 많아요. (This cell phone is lightweight and pretty, so it’s very popular.)
- 요리를 배우기 시작했는데 어렵기도 하고 재미있기도 해요. (I just started cooking lessons, and I think it’s hard and fun at the same time.)
- 외국으로 유학을 가게 돼서 기쁘기도 하고 조금 겁도 나요. (I’m happy and a bit scared about going overseas to study.)
- 휴일에는 친구를 만나기도 하고 집에서 쉬기도 해요. (On days off, I meet with friends and rest at home.)

**가:** 영하 씨가 쓴 소설이 드라마로 만들어졌다면서요? (A: I heard that the novel you wrote was re-made into a drama, Yeongha.)

**나:** 네, 다음 주부터 방송이 되는데 기쁘기도 하고 걱정이 되기도 해요. (B: Yes, it’s going to be aired next week, and I’m both pleased and worried at the same time.)

**가:** 오늘 파티에 갈 거야? (A: Are you going to the party tonight?)

**나:** 아니, 피곤하기도 하고 머리도 좀 아파서 안 가려고 해. (B: No, I’m a bit tired and I have a headache so I’m thinking about passing.)

**Note:** This can be used with concepts that are related and those that are opposite each other.

### 1. -을걸요/ㄹ걸요

**Meaning and Usage**
- This is used with an action or descriptive verb when a speaker makes a guess regarding something that has not happened or something that he/she is unsure about.

**Examples**

### 2. -거나 하면

**Meaning and Usage**
- This is used with an action or descriptive verb to express that when the situation in the preceding clause takes place, the situation in the following clause will follow. It is also used to make a request.

**Examples**

- 화면이 깨지거나 전화가 끊기거나 하면 언제든지 오세요. (If the screen cracks or the phone cuts off, come visit any time.)

**가:** 치수가 크거나 옷에 이상이 있거나 하면 다시 오세요. 언제든지 교환해 드립니다. (A: If the size is too big or there’s something wrong with the clothes, come visit us again. We’ll provide an exchange anytime.)

**나:** 네, 감사합니다. (B: Okay. Thank you.)

**Note:** More than two things can be listed by repeating ‘-거나’ or it can be used to express that there are more things involved than what is stated. The contents of the preceding clause is the purpose or cause of the contents of the following clause.

- 주말인데 다가 날씨가 좋아서 놀이공원에 사람이 많았을 텐데요. (There were probably tons of people at the amusement parks since it’s the weekend and the weather is so nice.)

**가:** 우산이 없으면 미리 연락을 하지. 그랬으면 데리러 갔을 텐데. (A: You should’ve called sooner if you didn’t have an umbrella. Then, I would’ve gone to pick you up.)

**나:** 가까운 거리니까 괜찮을 거라고 생각했어. (B: I thought it would be all right since it’s so close by.)

**가:** 어제 콘서트 재미있었어요? 못 가서 너무 아쉬웠어요. (A: Was the concert fun yesterday? I was so sad that I couldn’t make it.)

**나:** 네, 정말 좋았어요. 같이 갔으면 좋았을 텐데요. (B: Yes, it was great. It would’ve been better if you had come.)

### 2. -만 하다

**Meaning and Usage**
- This is used with a noun to express the size or degree of a person or product that is being compared.

**Examples**
- 우리 형은 키가 저만해요. (My older brother is as tall as me.)
- 이번에 새로 산 노트북은 이 공책만한데 기능이 아주 많아요. (The new laptop I purchased is as big as this notebook but with so many functions.)
- 미영이 동생은 배우인데 얼굴이 주먹만해요. (Miyeong’s sister is an actor and her face is as big as my fist.)
- 지나의 목소리가 모깃소리만해서 발표 내용이 잘 들리지 않았어요. (Jina's voice is as thin as a mosquito’s so it was hard to hear the contents of the presentation.)

**가:** 제임스 씨 고향은 얼마나 커요? (A: James, how big is your hometown?)

**나:** 우리 고향은 서울만한 것 같아요. 그렇게 크지 않아요. (B: My hometown is about as big as Seoul, I believe. It’s not that big.)

**가:** 새로 이사간 곳은 어때? (A: How’s the place you moved into?)

**나:** 위치는 나쁘지 않은데 방이 손바닥만해. (B: The location isn’t too bad, but the room is about as big as the palm of my hand.)
