### Lesson 1: Introduction
**<1> It's not hard at all**

**Dialogue**
**Jiang Shuying:** Hello, my name is Jiang Shuying. I am a new student in the Computer Engineering Department and my dream is to become an Information Technology specialist. I hope to get along well with all of you seniors and classmates.
**Senior:** Congratulations again on coming to our department. It’s been two weeks since we met at the orientation. College life must be hard since you’re just starting out.
**Jang Shuying:** It’s not hard at all. I’m doing well since all the seniors are so friendly and helpful. Thank you.
**Senior:** We’re only sharing what we ourselves went through. Here’s my phone number. If things become hard, give me a call.

**Vocabulary List**
*   기술 technology
*   꿈 dream
*   동기 same year peer
*   신입생 new student
*   전문가 specialist
*   학과 department
*   정보 information
*   입학하다 to enroll
*   강의 lecture
*   과목 subject
*   성적 grades
*   새내기 freshman
*   입학식 enrollment ceremony
*   장학금 scholarship
*   재학생 enrolled student
*   졸업생 graduate

**Grammar**
**1. -기는요**
*   **Meaning and Usage:** This is used with an action or descriptive verb to negate what the other person has said or when the speaker does not agree with what has just been said. When used in response to a compliment, it is an expression of humility.
*   **Examples:**
    *   일을 다 끝내기는요. 아직 시작도 못했는데요. I haven’t finished all the work. I haven’t even begun.
    *   숙제를 안 하기는요. 다 했는데 깜빡 잊고 안 가지고 왔어요. I have done the homework. I finished it but just forgot to bring it with me.
    *   시험이 쉽기는요. 아는 것보다 모르는 것이 더 많았어요. The test isn’t easy. There were more questions I didn’t know the answers to than the ones I did.
    *   한국말을 잘 하기는요. 쉬운 말밖에 못 해요. I’m not good at Korean. I can only speak simple phrases.
    *   가: 영수 씨, 발표 준비는 다 했어요? A: Yeongsu, are you finished preparing for your presentation?
    *   나: 다 하기는요. 아직 시작도 못했어요. B: I’m not finished. I haven’t even begun.
    *   가: 한국말을 참 잘하시네요. A: You’re so good at Korean.
    *   나: 잘 하기는요. 아직도 모르는 게 많아요. B: Not at all. There are so many things I still don’t know.

**2. ―을/ㄹ 뿐이다**
*   **Meaning and Usage:** This is used with an action or descriptive verb to show that there is nothing in addition to what has been stated. It also conveys a tone of diminished value or importance regarding the preceding content.
*   **Examples:**
    *   그 사람 이름만 알 뿐이에요. 다른 것은 잘 몰라요. I only know that person’s name. I don’t know anything else.
    *   건강이 좋아진 특별한 방법은 없어요. 아침마다 산책을 했을 뿐이에요. There isn’t any secret method to my health improving. All I did was take a walk every morning.
    *   고맙기는요, 시간이 있어서 조금 도왔을 뿐이에요. No need to thank me. I just helped out because I had some time to spare.
    *   미정 씨는 같은 반 친구일 뿐이에요. 제 여자친구가 아니에요. Mijeong is just a friend from class. She’s not my girlfriend.
    *   가: 영수 씨, 얼굴이 안 좋아 보여요. 어디 아프세요? A: Yeongsu, you don’t look well. Are you sick?
    *   나: 괜찮아요. 좀 피곤할 뿐이에요. B: I’m okay. I’m just tired.
    *   가: 도와주셔서 감사합니다. A: Thank you for your help.
    *   나: 감사하기는요. 제가 해야 할 일을 했을 뿐입니다. B: No need for thanks. I just did what I had to do.
*   **Note:** This can be used to speak of something you have done in a humble manner.

### Lesson 1: Introduction
**<2> The workload is relatively high**

**Dialogue**
**Director:** You must be nervous since it’s your first day at work. The workload at our company is relatively high, but there are many colleagues who will help you, Mr. Yeongsu Lee. Let’s do our best to get along.

**Youngsu Lee:** I diligently prepared myself in order to get a job at a trading company.
**Director:** That’s good. Things may seem unfamiliar at first, but you’ll get used to it in no time. If there’s anything you’re curious about, feel free to ask me.
**Youngsu Lee:** Thank you, Director. I’ll do my best.

**Vocabulary List**

*   최선 best
*   궁금하다 to be curious
*   긴장되다 to be nervous
*   낯설다 to be unfamiliar
*   뛰어나다 to be excellent
*   입사하다 to join a company
*   지내보다 to get along with
*   사회생활 social life
*   성별 gender
*   생년월일 date of birth
*   이력서 resume
*   자기소개 self-introduction
*   특기 special ability
*   학력 level of education
*   도전하다 to challenge
*   합격하다 to pass

**Grammar**
**1. ―기 위해서**
*   **Meaning and Usage:** This is used with an action verb to reveal the purpose or intent for doing something.
*   **Examples:**
    *   저는 세계 여행을 하기 위해서 외국어를 공부해요. I am learning a foreign language in order to travel the world.
    *   저는 선생님이 되기 위해서 교육학을 전공할 거예요. I will major in education in order to become a teacher.
    *   제 동생은 중국으로 유학을 가기 위해서 중국어를 공부하고 있어요. My younger sibling is studying Chinese in order to study abroad in China.
    *   이튼 씨는 친구들을 위해서 고향 음식을 만들었어요. Ethan made his hometown food for his friends.
    *   가: 우디 씨, 왜 그렇게 열심히 공부해요? A: Woody, why are you studying so hard?
    *   나: 공무원 시험에 합격하기 위해서 열심히 공부해요. B: I am studying hard in order to pass the civil service examination.
    *   가: 코넨 씨, 날마다 이렇게 운동하시는 거예요? A: Conan, do you exercise like this every day?
    *   나: 네, 저는 건강을 위해서 날마다 운동을 해요. B: Yes, I exercise every day for my health.
*   **Note:** This cannot be used with a descriptive verb. It can also be used as ‘-을/를 위해서’. It is a formal and literary expression.

**2. ―는/은/ㄴ 편이다**
*   **Meaning and Usage:** This is used with an action or descriptive verb to avoid speaking definitively about something and instead speak of it in relative terms.
*   **Form:**
    *   ‘-는 편이다’ is used with action verbs including ‘있다’ and ‘없다’ after the verb stem.
    *   ‘-ㄴ 편이다’ is used when the descriptive verb stem ends with a vowel or ‘ㄹ’.
    *   ‘-은 편이다’ is used when it ends with a consonant.
*   **Examples:**
    *   저는 한 달에 다섯 번 이상 극장에 가니까 자주 가는 편이에요. I go to the theater relatively often, considering that I go five times or more a month.
    *   코리 씨가 노래를 잘 못한다고요? 그 정도면 잘하는 편이에요. You think Corey doesn’t sing well? He sings relatively well.
    *   플릭 씨는 키가 큰 편이지요. Flick is pretty tall.
    *   안느 씨, 한국 음식을 처음 만들었는데 이 정도면 맛있는 편이지요. Anne, it’s your first time making Korean food and this is pretty good.
    *   가: 주디 씨, 토미 씨는 어떤 사람이에요? A: Judy, what kind of person is Tommy?
    *   나: 말이 별로 없는 편이지만 같이 있으면 재미있어요. B: He may seem relatively untalkative but he’s fun to be around.
    *   가: 날이 이렇게 더운데 집까지 30분이나 걸어가야 해요. 너무 멀어요. A: It’s so hot and I have to walk 30 minutes to get home. It’s too far.
    *   나: 그 정도면 가까운 편이지요. 저는 한 시간 이상 걸려요. B: That’s pretty close. It takes me more than an hour to get home.
*   **Note:** Action verbs are used together with adverbs that show the degree of something such as ‘자주’, ‘잘’, ‘많이.’

### Lesson 2: School life
**<1> I was just about to take a break**

**Dialogue**
**Nora:** Yuki, there’s a personal question I’d like to ask you. Are you busy right now?
**Yuki:** I’m okay. As a matter of fact, I was just about to take a break.
**Nora:** I want to work part-time in Korea, but I don’t know how to go about finding information.
**Yuki:** How about trying the school counseling office? If you go into the school website, there’s a schedule and information regarding counseling. Just choose a time slot that’s available and sign up for counseling.
**Nora:** Thank you. I’m always just receiving help from you.

**Vocabulary List**
*   개인적 personal
*   상담 counseling
*   상담실 counseling office
*   일정표 schedule
*   가능하다 to be possible
*   선택하다 to choose
*   이용하다 to use
*   그렇지 않아도 as a matter of fact
*   상담 내용 content of counseling
*   온라인 상담 online counseling
*   전화 상담 phone counseling
*   진학 상담 academic counseling
*   취업 상담 career counseling
*   면담하다 to have an interview
*   의논하다 to discuss

**Grammar**
**1. -으려던/려던 참이었다**
*   **Meaning and Usage:** This is used after an action verb stem to show that you were thinking of doing something. It is used to express that you happened to do something related to an action you were already considering doing.
*   **Examples:**
    *   저도 지금 점심을 먹으려던 참이었어요. I was just about to eat lunch, too.
    *   그렇지 않아도 네게 부탁하려던 참이었어. As a matter of fact, I was just about to ask you.
    *   저도 커피를 마시려던 참이었어요. 같이 가요. I was just about to drink coffee, too. Let’s go together.
    *   그렇지 않아도 노라 씨한테 연락하려던 참이었어요. As a matter of fact, I was just about to contact you, Nora.
    *   가: 3시 기차를 타려면 지금 출발해야 될 것 같은데요. A: I think we need to leave now in order to board the 3 o’clock train.
    *   나: 그렇지 않아도 이제 출발하려던 참이었어요. B: As a matter of fact, I was just about to leave.
    *   가: 에릭 씨, 지금 통화할 수 있어요? A: Are you available to talk on the phone right now, Eric?
    *   나: 네, 유키 씨, 저도 유키 씨에게 전화하려던 참이었어요. B: Yes, Yuki. I was just about to call you.

**2. -기만 하다**
*   **Meaning and Usage:** This is used with an action verb to show that no other action but one is being performed. ‘N을/를 하다’ can also be used interchangeably with ‘N만 하다’.
*   **Examples:**
    *   제 친구는 책을 사기만 하고 읽지는 않아요. My friend only buys books; she never reads them.
    *   아이가 밥도 안 먹고 울기만 해요. The baby doesn’t even eat and only cries.
    *   어제 잠을 못 자서 영화를 볼 때 계속 졸기만 했어요. I didn’t get much sleep last night, so I just kept falling asleep during the movie.
    *   주말에 저는 쉬지도 않고 공부만 했어요. I had no time to rest; I only studied during the weekend.
    *   가: 어머니께서 요리를 하실 때 마리아 씨도 같이 했어요? A: Did you help your mother with the cooking, Maria?
    *   나: 아니요, 저는 옆에서 보기만 했어요. B: No, I just watched from the side.
    *   가: 일요일에 밀린 집안일을 좀 했어요? A: Were you able to get some overdue chores done on Sunday?
    *   나: 아니요, 너무 피곤해서 쉬기만 했어요. B: No, I was too tired so I just rested.

### Lesson 2: School life
**<2> If you want to enjoy your school life, you need to sign up for a club**

**Dialogue**
**Hana:** What are those people gathered over there doing? It looks so fun.
**Senior:** It’s a photography club. A close friend of mine asked me to join with him so I signed up, too, last year. Are you interested in photography?
**Hana:** Yes, I was always interested, starting in high school. What kind of activities do you usually do?
**Senior:** We go throughout the country and take pictures. Then, we organize the pictures that we each took and hold an exhibition once a year.
**Hana:** Sounds fun. But I’m having a hard time just keeping up with the courses for my major. Do you think I’ll be able to juggle club activities, too?
**Senior:** You’ll be busy, but if you want to enjoy your school life, you need to sign up for a club.

**Vocabulary List**

*   가입하다 to sign up for
*   돌아다니다 to go around
*   친하다 to be close
*   국악 Korean traditional music
*   발표회 presentation
*   연주회 performance
*   합창 chorus
*   회장 president

*   활동하다 to do an activity

**Grammar**
**1. ―어야지요/아야지요/여야지요**
*   **Meaning and Usage:** This is used with an action verb to express a need for the other party or someone else to carry out an action.
*   **Examples:**
    *   약속을 했으면 지켜야지요. If you make a promise, you need to keep it.
    *   담배는 밖에 나가서 피워야지. You need to smoke outside.
    *   2시까지 도착하려면 지금 출발해야지요. You need to leave now if you want to arrive by 2 p.m.
    *   산에 올라갈 때는 불편한 신발을 신지 말아야지. You can’t wear uncomfortable shoes when climbing a mountain.
    *   가: 영수 씨가 저에게만 한 이야기를 실수로 다른 사람에게 말했어요. A: I accidentally spilled the beans on the secret that Yeongsu only shared with me.
    *   나: 비밀 이야기는 다른 사람에게 말하지 말아야지요. B: You shouldn’t tell others a secret (that’s been told to you).
*   **Note:** When used in the first person, this is used to express a personal determination or vow that has been made. When used with a descriptive verb about someone or something, it expresses that what is being described must possess a certain quality.

**2. ―어/아/여 보이다**
*   **Meaning and Usage:** This is used with a descriptive verb when the speaker is expressing feelings or speculation about someone or something he/she observed.
*   **Examples:**
    *   더러웠던 방을 청소하니까 방이 넓어 보여요. The room looks bigger after I cleaned up the mess.
    *   흐엉 씨는 나이보다 어려 보여요. Huong, you look younger than your age.
    *   요즘 일이 많아요? 피곤해 보여요. Are you busy these days? You look tired.
    *   어제 친척 결혼식에 갔는데 신랑과 신부가 아주 행복해 보였어요. I attended a relative’s wedding yesterday and the bride and groom looked so happy.
    *   가: 이 영화가 재미있어 보이는데 우리 이 영화를 볼까요? A: This movie looks fun. Do you want to watch it?
    *   나: 좋아요. 저도 보고 싶었던 거예요. B: Sure. I’ve been wanting to watch it, too.
    *   가: 정말 예쁜 케이크네요. 맛있을 것 같아요. A: It’s a really pretty cake. I think it’ll be tasty.
    *   나: 네, 저도 맛있어 보여서 샀어요. B: Yes, I bought it because it looked tasty.
*   **Note:** This cannot be used with an action verb.

### Lesson 3: Home
**<1> Perhaps it’s because I’ve grown accustomed to them, but I like apartments**

**Dialogue**
**Eric:** Huong, what kind of home do you want to live in if you continue to live in Korea?
**Huong:** Um, I want to live in a house where I can grow my own garden.
**Eric:** Won’t it be hard to maintain?
**Huong:** It may be hard to maintain, but I think it’ll be nice because it’s a space just for our family. We can go out to the garden anytime and get a breath of fresh air.
**Eric:** Perhaps it’s because I’ve grown accustomed to them, but I like apartments. It’s also convenient because they come equipped with many different facilities.

**Vocabulary List**
*   공간 space
*   정원 garden
*   갖추다¹ to be equipped with
*   가꾸다 to grow
*   관리하다 to maintain
*   상쾌하다 to be fresh
*   단독주택 house
*   고층 high-rise
*   교육환경 educational environment
*   자연환경 natural environment
*   저층 low-rise
*   편리하다 to be convenient
*   환하다 to be bright
*   편의시설 convenience facility

**Grammar**
**1. ―는다면/ㄴ다면/다면**
*   **Meaning and Usage:** This is used with an action or descriptive verb when supposing that something will happen. It is used when there is a low possibility of it actually happening.
*   **Form:**
    *   ‘-ㄴ다면’ is used when the action verb stem ends in ‘ㄹ’ or a vowel.
    *   ‘-는다면’ is used when it ends in a consonant.
    *   ‘-다면’ is used after the descriptive verb stem.
*   **Examples:**
    *   마음에 드는 사람을 찾는다면 결혼할 거예요. If I find someone I like, I’m going to marry him/her.
    *   만약 10억이 생긴다면 집을 사고 싶어요. If I got a billion dollars, I would want to buy a house.
    *   시험이 없다면 정말 행복할 것 같아요. If there were no tests, I think I would be so happy.
    *   내가 너라면 담배를 피우지 않겠어. If I were you, I wouldn’t smoke.
    *   가: 뭐든지 만들 수 있다면 뭘 만들고 싶어요? A: If you could make anything, what would you want to make?
    *   나: 뭐든지 만들 수 있다면 젊어지는 약을 만들고 싶어요. B: If I could make anything, I would want to make a potion that makes people younger.
    *   가: 과거로 돌아갈 수 있다면 언제로 돌아가고 싶어? A: If you could go back in time, when would you want to return to?
    *   나: 유치원 때로 돌아가고 싶어. 그때는 친구만 있으면 행복했어. B: I want to return to kindergarten. Back then, all I needed was friends to be happy.

**2. ―어서/아서/여서 그런지**
*   **Meaning and Usage:** This is used with an action or descriptive verb to express the cause or reason why the speaker has made an assumption.
*   **Examples:**
    *   요즘 과로해서 그런지 자주 머리가 아파요. Perhaps it’s because I’m overworked these days, but my head frequently hurts.
    *   근처에 공원이 있어서 그런지 집값이 비싸요. Perhaps it’s because there’s a park nearby, but the houses here are expensive.
    *   요즘 날씨가 더워서 그런지 입맛이 없어요. Perhaps it’s because it’s so hot out these days, but I don’t have an appetite.
    *   유명한 관광지여서 그런지 비가 와도 사람이 많군요. Perhaps it’s because it’s a famous tourist attraction, but there are a lot of people even when it rains.
    *   가: 존 씨는 참 똑똑한 것 같아요. A: I think John is very smart.
    *   나: 책을 많이 읽어서 그런지 아는 것이 많아요. B: Perhaps it’s because he reads a lot, but he’s well-informed.
    *   가: 이 가방은 디자인은 예쁜데 너무 비싼 것 같아요. A: The bag’s design is very pretty, but I think it’s too expensive.
    *   나: 너무 비싸서 그런지 사람들도 구경만 하고 안 사네요. B: Perhaps it’s because it’s too expensive, but people just look and don’t buy.

### Lesson 3: Home
**<2> The school can be seen from here, too**

**Dialogue**
**Olivia:** I like the room because it’s spacious and bright. The school can be seen from here, too.
**Real estate agent:** Yes, the view is great and it’s clean since newlyweds were the former residents. There’s also good circulation so it’s cool.
**Olivia:** You’re sure that there are no problems with the water, air-conditioning, and heating systems, right?
**Real estate agent:** Of course. If there’s a problem, the homeowner will certainly fix it for you, so don’t worry.
**Olivia:** I like the house. When can I move in?
**Real estate agent:** The house is empty, so as soon as you draw up the contract, you can move in anytime.

**Vocabulary List**
*   난방 heating
*   냉방 air-conditioning
*   부동산 real estate agency
*   수도 water
*   신혼부부 newlyweds
*   통하다 to circulate
*   확실하다 to be sure
*   당연히 certainly
*   걸리다 to take (time)

**Grammar**
**1. 피동 (Passive Voice)**
*   **Meaning and Usage:** The passive voice is used when a movement or action takes place naturally as a result of an external situation. A verb that serves this function is called a passive verb.
*   **Form:** The passive voice takes the form of an action verb stem followed by ‘-이-, -히-, -리-, -기-’, ‘-어지다.’
*   **Examples:**
    *   -이-: 보이다, 쌓이다, 놓이다, 쓰이다, 바뀌다
    *   -히-: 닫히다, 잡히다, 찍히다, 꽂히다, 먹히다
    *   -리-: 들리다, 열리다, 걸리다, 팔리다
    *   -기-: 끊기다, 씻기다, 쫓기다, 안기다
